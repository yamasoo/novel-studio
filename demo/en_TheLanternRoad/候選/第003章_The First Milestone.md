# 第3章 The First Milestone

Wren left before dawn, with the satchel on her back and Hob lit in her fist, and the silver line led her out through the north gate as if it had always been there.

It ran across wet fields in one long straight stroke, twenty paces of it visible ahead of her and twenty behind. She counted without meaning to. Four hundred and twelve paces to the first ditch. Nine hundred and six to the hedge. At twelve hundred the line bent around a rise, and on the far side the ground gave way to a stone.

It was half-sunk, tilted, furred with moss, and someone had carved a lantern into its face. Beneath the lantern, a single numeral: 1.

“Milestone,” said Hob. “The first of the road.”

“There’s no road.”

“Noted.”

She knelt and brushed the moss away with her thumb. The carving was deep and old, and the lantern was a good one: handle, cracked left pane, everything. She held Hob up beside it. He said nothing, which was how she knew.

Next to the stone a stake stood in the turf. Wren had seen ten thousand of them: white-painted ash with a Guild number in black. This one was painted fresh. The paint was still tacky.

“The Guild’s been here,” she said, and her voice came out smaller than she meant. “This week.”

“Wren.”

“They know the road is here. All of them know, and they—”

“Wren.” The glass had gone milky, like breath on a window. “Someone near me is lying.”

She stood very still. “I’m fine,” she said.

The fog thickened.

“Fine,” Wren said again, more carefully, “is a strong word.” The glass cleared a little.

She crouched and turned the stake to the light. There was no Guild number. Instead, cut into the wood with a pocketknife, were two letters she would have known in a dark room: *O.V.*
