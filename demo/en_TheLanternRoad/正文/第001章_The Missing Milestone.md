# 第1章 The Missing Milestone

The shop had been quiet for twenty-one days, and Wren Alder had counted every one of them.

She counted the way she counted everything: in paces, in pen strokes, in the number of times the bell over the door had failed to ring. Today the number was zero again. She bent over the town map of Hollin Cross and inked the last of the wall, one careful tick at a time.

The bell rang.

“Miss Alder.” The woman in the doorway wore Guild grey and carried a rolled warrant like a baker carrying a loaf. “Dessa Pike, Surveyors’ Guild. I’m here for Master Vale’s survey books.”

“He isn’t here.”

“I know. That’s rather why I am.” Dessa Pike smiled with her mouth only. “The Guild keeps all field records. Thirty years of them, in this shop.”

Wren wiped her left thumb on her apron. The ink had stained it blue-black long ago and would not come off. “They’re on the second shelf.”

“Thank you.” Dessa’s eyes drifted to the old map pinned beside the stove. “There’s a rumor your master surveyed a trade road past Hollin Cross. I told the Warden the Guild has no record of any such road. Did I tell him wrongly?”

“The Guild has no record,” Wren said. It was true. She had checked.

Dessa took the books, thirty-six of them, in three trips. Wren counted those too. When the bell finally rang shut behind her, the shop felt like a held breath let go.

She went to put the kettle on and stopped, because the old map’s margin was in front of her, and she had looked at it every day for four years and never really seen it. Milestones ran down the edge in Orrin’s slanting hand: 38, 39, 40, 42, 43.

“Forty-one,” she said aloud.

Nothing answered. She turned to the stove, and behind it, where the wall met the bricks, her foot found something that rang.

A lantern lay there in a strip of oilcloth, dented brass with a cracked left pane. A note was tied to the handle in Orrin’s slanting hand: *Light it with a name you have earned. —O.*

She lifted it. It was heavier than it looked, and warmer than anything left behind a stove for three weeks had a right to be.

“You’re not Orrin,” said the lantern.

Wren did not drop it. She was rather proud of that, afterward.

“No,” she said.

“Hm. Noted.” The cracked pane gave a faint, dry glint. “Then which road did he tell you to forget?”
