# 第2章 Names You Have Walked

The lantern would not light.

Wren had tried everything a reasonable person tries with a lantern: a match, a spill from the stove, and finally the flat of her hand over the dark wick, which was not reasonable and was somehow warm.

“It isn’t the flame,” said Hob. He had given his name after the third match. “I light for a name, not a spark.”

“What name?”

“A place you have stood in. Not read about. Not drawn.”

“I’ve drawn every place in the Marches.”

“Noted. Try again.”

“Hollin Cross.” Nothing. “Greywater.” The glass stayed dark, and Hob said, “You’ve never been to Greywater,” in the tone of someone confirming a bill.

Wren sat down on the shop floor. She made herself think of what her feet knew instead of her pen: the market, the north gate, the dairy lane. Then, without warning, a summer when she was twelve, Orrin’s tripod on her shoulder and a river cold to her knees.

“Pellman’s Ford,” she said.

The wick caught.

It was not a flame, exactly. The light was low and gold and reached about twenty paces in every direction. But when she carried it to the north window and held it to the glass, the fields beyond the town wall had a line in them: thin, silver, running north-east as straight as a ruled stroke.

“Is that—”

“A road,” said Hob. “Not on any map.”

“It should be on one.” She heard herself say it, and the cold arrived a second later, climbing her arms as if she had walked into the river again. “I’ve drawn that edge of the map a hundred times. There’s nothing there.”

“Is there not?” said Hob, gently, for him.

She set the lantern very carefully on the drawing table. Last spring Orrin had handed her the master map and said, *Rub out that line between the Cross and the Greywater, Alder, and draw fields. A smudge. Copy it clean.* She had. She had done it without a question, because he was Orrin, and because copying clean was the one thing she did well.

She was hungry, suddenly, and cold, and she had a satchel to pack.

Outside the window, the road she had rubbed out with her own hand ran on into the dark, and it was waiting.
