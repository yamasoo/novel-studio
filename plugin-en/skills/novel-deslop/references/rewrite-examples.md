# Rewrite examples

All original. Each pair is before → after with one line on what changed. Learn the move; do not copy the sentences into a manuscript.

---

## 1. Stacked micro-expressions

**Before**
> Her eyes widened slightly and something flickered across her face. A small smile tugged at the corner of her lips as she let out a breath she hadn't realized she was holding.

**After**
> Ines blinked, then laughed. "You heard about that already?"

**Why**: four stock gestures in a row. Keep one reaction and let her line show the rest.

---

## 2. Telling the emotion

**Before**
> Callum felt a wave of crushing despair wash over him. He realized everything he had worked for was gone.

**After**
> Callum sat on the edge of the bed until the tea went from hot to warm to cold. He didn't pick up the letter again.

**Why**: the feeling is never named; time and an untouched object carry it.

---

## 3. Not X but Y

**Before**
> It wasn't fear that gripped her. It was fury. Not at him, but at herself.

**After**
> She was angry at herself. Three years, and she had missed it.

**Why**: two knocked-down targets removed. The conclusion plus one concrete reason.

---

## 4. Reflective chapter ending

**Before**
> She closed the door behind her, knowing that whatever came next, nothing would ever be the same again. The storm was only beginning.

**After**
> She closed the door and stood there with the key still in her hand, listening to the stairwell light click off on its timer.

**Why**: ends on an image, not a verdict or a forecast.

---

## 5. Brochure dialogue

**Before**
> "I understand that you are upset," Mara said calmly. "I want you to know that I never intended to hurt you, and I'm willing to talk about it whenever you are ready."
> "Thank you for saying that," Jonas replied. "I appreciate your honesty, and I need some time to process."

**After**
> "You're angry," Mara said.
> Jonas turned the key over in his fingers. "Ask me Thursday."

**Why**: nobody states their feelings in full sentences. Subtext, a small action, a deferral.

---

## 6. Tidy triplet and aphorism

**Before**
> He was tired, he was hungry, and he was alone. Some journeys, he thought, are not meant to be finished.

**After**
> The thermos was empty and the nearest light was two ridges away. He started walking anyway.

**Why**: the three-beat list and the quotable line are replaced by two specifics and a decision.

---

## 7. Stock body clichés

**Before**
> A shiver ran down her spine and her heart hammered in her chest as the air seemed to leave the room.

**After**
> She counted the exits. One. The man in the doorway was between her and it.

**Why**: the body cliché is replaced by what this character does when afraid: she counts.

---

## 8. Burst of fragments

**Before**
> He turned. Nothing. A sound. Footsteps. Closer. Then silence.

**After**
> He turned, but the corridor was empty. Somewhere behind the wall a pair of shoes crossed the floor, slowly, and stopped.

**Why**: the staccato was faking tension. One full sentence with one real sound.
